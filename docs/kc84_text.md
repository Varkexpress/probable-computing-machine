*The tall one brings clothes. A grey T-shirt, far too big, and soft trousers with a drawstring. He puts them on the end of the bed, folded, and says that he will be outside.*

*The clothes you came in are not on the chair. They were not yours either. You know that, and you have put it somewhere you do not look.*

*He goes out, and he shuts the door. You hear him walk away from it, down the corridor, loudly, all the way to the end.*

**Misaki:** (One: he left. Two: he shut the door. Three: they do not do that. They do not leave when you—)

*You stop the sum there. It was going to come out to something, and you do not need it to.*

- ▸ Change, fast, facing the door

*You change in about nine seconds, facing the door, with the good hand. The T-shirt comes to your knees. It smells of soap, and of nothing else, and you notice yourself checking.*

**Misaki:** (It is only a rule: this one leaves the room. Fine. It does not mean anything.)

*The tall one does your hand. He will not do anything else. A woman in a grey cardigan does the rest, and he waits in the corridor while she does it, with the door shut.*

*When he has to reach the drip, he says: “I need to move the blanket by the width of a hand. May I?”*

**Misaki:** (He asked. The tall one asked me. New rule: this one asks first.)

*Then the other thought comes up under it, cold, in the voice you use on a roof: the ones who ask are the ones who want to hear you say yes.*

- ▸ Say yes

*You nod, and keep nodding, to anything.*

**Misaki:** Yes. Of course. Whatever you want.

*He does not move the blanket. He stands there with his hand in the air for a moment, and then he goes and fetches the woman in the cardigan to do it, and waits outside.*

**Misaki:** (Was that the wrong answer? How can yes be the wrong answer?)

*It is V’s house, and the tall one is V. You have had that for a day. This morning the rest of it arrives.*

*He carried you out of that building. He saw what you had on. He is a man with forty pages on you, who writes everything down, in ink, with the date.*

**Misaki:** (He has it. It is in the book. He has a line for it in fountain pen, under my name, with the date.)

*Of everybody in this city, the one who knows is the one who swore to kill you. You can think of nothing he could do to you on a steel table that is worse than him having that written down.*

*When he brings the tray, you ask him. You ask it sideways.*

**Misaki:** What did you write in the book, V? About the river.

**V:** The date. The address. That you were recovered alive.

**Misaki:** That is all?

**V:** That is all that I will ever write of it. The rest does not belong to my book. It is yours, and I have no claim on it.

- ▸ “good. bc there’s nothing else to write”

**V:** As you say.

*He says it without any weight on it at all. You have heard him lie to a board of directors, in Angel’s videos, and he was much better at it then.*

**Misaki:** (He knows. He knows, and he is not going to use it. Why is he not going to use it?)

- ▸ “cool. whatever. soup’s cold”

**V:** I will warm it.

*He takes the bowl away and warms it, and brings it back, and does not once look at you below the chin. You notice that he never has, not since you knew him. You had put it down to manners.*

*The doctor has told you nothing. She is not permitted to, and you would think less of her if she did.*

*She has asked you for a camera, a ruler, a card to write the date on, a white box that seals, and a lock for the small fridge. You have been at this work for eleven years. You know what that list is for.*

*Your book has had four men in it for whom you kept the steel table and did not hurry. You know what they do, because you have read the files. You know what it looks like afterwards, because you have sat with the people it was done to while they did not say it.*

**V:** (I wrote SLOW at the top of their page. I meant the cold room. I meant the straps.)

**V:** (I would have had them where those four were. One more room in which they could not have said no, and I would have called it law.)

*You go up to the study and take the plan out of the drawer: the trailer from the air, their week in columns, the three ways. The plan is not the book. You are permitted to burn a plan.*

*You burn it in the grate, a sheet at a time, and hold each one until it reaches your fingers.*

*They thank you for the water. It is the pleasant voice, the careful one, with the eyes on your hands.*

*You have despised that voice for three days. You took it for cowardice, in a killer who never once showed any on a roof.*

**V:** (It is not cowardice. It is a method. It is what one says, and how one holds the face, so that the one with the keys will be finished sooner.)

**V:** (They learned it in four days in that building. Or they have had it since before they could write a month on a form, and took it out again when it was needed. I cannot tell which, and I find that I cannot bear either.)

*By your law, what is done is owed back to those who did it, in kind. You have names for some of them, and you will have the rest.*

**V:** (And that settles one account. There is another, and nothing I take out of those men will go back into them. The book is for eyes and for hands. It has no column for this.)

*You say “You are welcome”, because not to answer would frighten them. It is the foulest thing you have ever had in your mouth.*

*The doctor is required to ask one question, once a day. You know when she asks it, because the vent in the study carries.*

*For three days there has been no answer that you could hear. Today there is. It is loud, and it is rude, and it is the first thing they have said in their own voice since the river.*

**Misaki:** NOTHING HAPPENED. Write that down, and put quotation marks on it if you like.

**V:** (It is their own voice. It is a lie. I was glad to hear it, and I will have to carry having been glad.)

*It is not your question. You may not ask it, and you may not have the answer, and you ought not to be sitting where you can hear it refused.*

- ▸ Screw the vent shut

*You fetch a screwdriver and a square of board, and you shut the vent over the desk. You heard them ask for the light through it, and you heard them laugh at nothing through it, and you will not hear either again.*

**V:** (What they say in that room is theirs. I have taken enough from them in writing.)

- ▸ Leave the study to them at that hour

*You leave the vent as it is, and you mark the hour. Every day after, at that hour, you are in the kennel, at the far end of the house, with the hose running.*

**V:** (The hounds have never been so clean. They do not know what they are being washed for.)

*The tall one has a bowl and a bottle and a pair of tweezers. He is going to do the wrists, where the ties went through.*

*He starts at the top. That is wrong. If you leave the edge, it seals over dirty and goes bad in a week. You have had that; you were about eleven.*

**Misaki:** (Do not tell the guard how to do his job. …He is doing it wrong, though.)

*You take the tweezers out of his hand with your good one, without a word, and show him: under the edge, lift, then flush.*

**Misaki:** You have to go under the edge. Lift it, then flush. Otherwise it seals dirty, and you’re back here in a week.

*He stops with the tweezers in the air. He looks at your wrists, and then at your face, and for a moment you think he is going to be sick in the bowl.*

**Misaki:** (What? It is only wrists. They are not even the worst I have had.)

*He does it your way. He is good with his hands, once he has been told.*

*He looks at your ribs today. He has to cut the old strapping off to do it, and when it comes away he makes a small sound through his nose.*

*You know what is under there. A boot, three or four times, from the left, by somebody right-footed. It is every colour. You have seen it, and you were not interested.*

**Misaki:** (He is annoyed; look at his face. He is annoyed at the state of it.)

*Of course he is. He is the careful one. The other shift did it sloppily and left him the mess, and a careful man hates a botched job.*

*You would tell him so, if the voice would come.*

**Misaki:** Yes. They were messy about it. That is not your fault.

*He puts both hands flat on the edge of the bed and keeps them there for a while. Then he straps you up again, so evenly that you could rule a line along it.*

*You know him now. So when he does the hand this morning and his face does that thing again, you say so.*

**Misaki:** What? You have done worse than this, V. You took eight fingers off a man.

**V:** I did. In order, and for cause.

**Misaki:** So why does this bother you? It is a hand. That is the third time for that one, and it sets fine.

**V:** Because it was done badly, to no purpose, by men who were bored.

**Misaki:** So you are a snob about it. About torture.

**V:** Yes. It appears that I am.

*It is the funniest thing you have heard in a fortnight. The man with the cold room is put off his breakfast by your ribs.*

**Misaki:** (…Nobody has been disgusted on my account before. Is that what that is?)

*You do the treatment yourself. You trust nobody else with a hand.*

*You know how a hand is broken. You have broken them on the steel table, in order, each bone answering a charge, and stopped when the sum came out.*

*This one was stamped on. A heel, or a car door, by somebody who was passing. The ribs were kicked from the left by a right-footed man who was not aiming. The wrists were put in cable ties and then left, until the plastic went through the skin by itself.*

**V:** (There is no order in it. There is no charge. Not one mark on them was for anything.)

**V:** (It disgusts me. I had not expected to be capable of that. I jointed the card man, and I am standing over a kicked rib and I find it obscene.)

*You had meant to do worse. It is in your own hand, with SLOW written across the top.*

**V:** (Mine would have been exact, and for cause. I have told myself for eleven years that this is a difference. I am no longer certain that the one on the table would have been able to tell.)

*You wash your hands before you touch them, and again afterwards, and you cannot have said which of the two was for whom.*

*You set the hand. They watch you do it as one watches a man change a tyre.*

*They do not speak. They turn the wrist for you before you ask, to the exact angle, and hold it there. They have been through this before, and they know where it goes.*

**Misaki:** That is the third time for that one. It sets fine. Do the thumb first, or the rest won’t sit.

*They are right about the thumb.*

*The films are on the lightbox. The new breaks are white and sharp. Behind them, further up, there are old ones: an arm that knit crooked, two ribs, a collarbone. None of them was ever set. The newest of those is from before they were nine.*

**V:** (Somebody watched a child carry a broken arm about until it healed bent.)

**V:** (I have despised them for feeling nothing about a doorman. They feel nothing about this either. It is the same skill. They were taught it before they could write the name of a month.)

*You keep four animals that were used like that. You took each of them in and wrote nothing against it. Against this one you wrote forty pages.*

**V:** (I find the old breaks more disgusting than the new. I am aware of what that says about where my disgust has gone, and I have not given it leave.)

*There is no scale in the house for a person. You carry them to the kennel and weigh them on the one for the hounds.*

*They weigh less than the grey hound. They find this extremely funny, and tell her so.*

*They had four days on water, and on whatever came through a slot. There is a tooth gone at the back, on the left; they showed you the gap with a finger, quite cheerfully, as proof of something.*

**V:** (They were not even starved for a purpose. Nobody decided upon it. They were forgotten, between one shift and the next.)

**V:** (I feed what is in my keeping. I fed the card man, and fed him well, to the last night. It is not kindness. A thing that I hold is held properly, or I am no better than a man with a van.)

*You are aware that this is a butcher’s objection. You are disgusted as a tradesman is, at bad work. You had thought that it would stop there, at the workmanship.*

**V:** (It has not stopped there. I do not want it. I did not ask to be made sick on behalf of something in my own book.)

*You cook. Broth first, then egg, then a little rice, in that order, a spoon at a time, as you would for an animal brought in off a chain. They eat all of it and ask whether there is any ham.*
