## What was wrong, and what this does about it

The withdrawn material had both of them explaining their own feelings at length, quietly, and it was mostly about
what the captivity did. `docs/voice_sample.md` has four people who enjoy the work, are good at it, and are funny
because they are dangerous. `docs/notes_kc73.md` says what these two fight about, and that neither ever stops
believing it:

- **V**: Misaki is wanton. No code, kills for rent, does not ask what the mark did, leaves collateral.
- **Misaki**: V is a rich man with a hobby who calls it law. Nobody elected him. The landlord does not take morals.

So every scene here is that argument, carried on by things they do to each other: money, jobs, the ledger, the
chainsaw. Misaki is loud and wins on points. V is exact and wins on facts. Nobody is talked round. Where either of
them shifts, it is shown by an act that they at once deny (forty-one dollars at a school office; “as it happens” in
the ledger; a quarter of a million turned down “as a pride thing”).

## Misaki's names for V

“grandpa” is gone from kc73–kc94 (about thirty lines). It read as fond, and it had nothing to do with the war.

- **“ur honour”** (upper tiers “your honour”): the staple, from the oath on. It is the quarrel in two words: he has
  made himself a judge. **“moneybags”** when it is about money.
- **Pet names are for a lover.** In the voice sample “babe” and “sugar honey dumpling” are what Misaki calls the
  person they love. So V gets none, anywhere, until a year on: in the lovers ending “sugar honey dumpling” gets out at
  breakfast and V writes it in the ledger with the date; in the rivals ending “babe” crosses four lanes of a bowling
  alley and Misaki denies it.
- Scene id `g_grandpa` (kc86) keeps its id for old saves; its title is now “Call him ‘ur honour’”.
- The normal route (kc22, td9, td18) still has “grandpa”. It was not touched.
