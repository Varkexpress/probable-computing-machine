## V: seven times, by seven doors

He is never talked out of it and never reasons his way out. Each time he gets a little way, and his body refuses
before he does. He despises them throughout; the last line of the last scene says so.

1. **The law.** It is due, as a bill is due. He lays the scalpel across their name and gets up, in the order his body
   knows. At the head of the back stair his legs stop. He sits on the top step with his head between his knees.
2. **Mercy.** They are asleep, and will wake into the second of not knowing which building. He has put down four of
   the eleven himself. His hand is over the place under the jaw before he hears that he is using the voice he uses on
   hounds. A hound cannot be asked; this one can, and he was arranging not to hear the answer. Sick in the corridor.
   “The law at least does not pretend to be fond of anybody.”
3. **Anger.** They thank him for the soup. Somebody has taken his enemy and left this. *Kill it.* He sees that he
   wants to destroy the evidence. He breaks a bowl, the first thing broken in anger since he was a boy, and picks up
   every piece.
4. **The hands.** He is thinking about gauze, and his left hand takes the hold that presents an arm to the blade, and
   his right goes to his collar. He scalds his hands under the tap and has Ronin do the next dressing.
5. **A throat offered.** He drops a dish; they go flat with their chin up. Something older than his law leaps. He has
   a foot forward. He is sick in the kidney dish, on their floor, in front of them, and knows how they will read it.
6. **The tidy book.** Two in the morning; every page is ruled off but theirs. Nobody would ask. He writes the upright
   of the T of TOTAL and cannot breathe. → `S.f.e2lTotal` `blot` (the only blot in the book) / `T` (he leaves the
   stroke).
7. **Once more.** They are themselves, scowling at him with pink stars on their nails; now it would be fair. The
   thought is complete and there is nothing under it. Only the sickness, which he recognises. This one waits until
   Misaki is clear.

## Misaki: what they want

- Cell: **dead, and alive** (they want him dead and need him alive; they cannot decide whether to kill somebody, the
  one question they could always answer); **over** (they beg a door for it, and when the steps come would give
  anything for him to turn round, and afterwards are nearly disappointed); **the hatred** (it has gone thin, and if it
  goes then he has had that too; they say “I’m going to kill you somewhere stupid” forty times, as a prayer).
- Clear: **owing** (they would like to thank him once in their own voice, and the words are used up); **the car park**
  (the old job will not run: he kneels in it; and they find they hope he still means to, because the alternative is
  pity).

The same text plays under both aftermath settings.
