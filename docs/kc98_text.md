## The last night

*Night. The bulb. The shoes in the passage, going up and down. He walks when he cannot sleep, and he never can.*

*You have done the sums. You always do the sums. He has never killed a thing that was not well, and you are not going to be well. So he will wait. He is the most patient man in the city. He will keep you in this bed, on this bag, until there is enough of you to answer for a doorman, and there is not going to be enough.*

*You go through what you still have, as you would go through a bag before a job. The spoon; you have not worked on it in days. The list, which is down to one line, and you could not swear to that one. A paper cup, washed, that you do not know why you kept.*

*You do not have the hours. They took the hours, and whatever was in them. You became what you became so that nobody would own any part of you again. You learned the wind and the drop and what a man is worth; you were cheap, and fast, and nobody’s. And for days you were somebody’s, and you were not even there for it.*

*They broke what they wanted to break. Some of it will set crooked. The hand is not going to hold a fore-end again; you have known that for a week and not said it. A contractor who cannot shoot is a person who owes rent.*

*The worst of it is that the war has gone. You have looked. You had a whole war: a rich man with a hobby, and a car park, and a promise that you made with your whole chest in front of everybody. It was the best thing you ever had, and you cannot find the door to it. You feel like a room that somebody has moved out of.*

*You think about Ronin, running a book on the two of you so that he would not have to drive over and look. Angel, and her list. You hold it up, and it is somebody else’s: a photograph of a town where you used to live.*

## V

*The shoes stop in the passage at four, because the breathing on the other side of the door has changed, and he has listened to that breathing for nine nights with the ledger shut on his knee.*

*He is through the door before the book has reached the floor. He has the bag. He has the light. He is fast, and he is very good, and he is too late by minutes, not hours.*

*They are looking at the door. The face is not afraid. It is the face of somebody who has worked a thing out a second too late, on a roof, when the wind has changed. He has had that face in his field glasses. He knows what it means.*

*He swore to kill them. He wrote it down, and he read it out, and it is in ink. He stands in the room marked LAUNDRY with their wrist in his hand and understands that it was the one thing about him on which they relied.*

## V, after (seven mornings)

### 1

*He takes the infirmary door off its hinges and leans it against the wall of the passage, so that the room cannot be shut again.*

*Then he goes up to the study and opens the ledger at their page. Every page in that book ends in the same way: a date, and a total. He has done it forty-one times.*

*He sits with the pen for a long while. He is an honest bookkeeper. He writes the date. Under TOTAL he writes: “Not collected.”*

**V:** (It is the only page in the book that I did not finish. I shall leave the book open at it. Let it be the first thing that anybody sees who comes into this room for me.)

### 2

*Ronin comes on the third day, with the crowbar and the book.*

**Ronin:** Whole city figures you did it. Hale figures you did it. I had a guy in a bar tell me you did it, an’ he’s in the hospital.

**V:** I said that I would. Before witnesses.

**Ronin:** Yeah, you did. An’ I told ’em you couldn’t. Stood up in a room an’ said my friend the serial killer couldn’t do it. You know how that sounds?

*He puts the book on the kitchen island. It has a rubber band round it. He has ruled a line under the last page.*

**Ronin:** Book’s closed. Everybody gets their stake back. I had four-to-one you’d never. I was right, an’ I ain’t collectin’.

*He stands there. Then he puts the crowbar through the kitchen table, once, to the floor, and leaves it standing in it, and goes out. V does not have it removed.*

### 3

*Angel comes with no make-up on, and a cactus in a pot.*

**Angel:** nobody else knows how to keep it. you watered it once and they nearly killed you for it. so don’t water it.

**V:** I shall not water it.

**Angel:** they left the rifle to me. the truck to ronin. you’re not in it. i checked twice. i want you to know they didn’t leave you anything, and i’m giving you this anyway, because i think they’d have hated that.

*He puts it on the sill in the spare room, which was never slept in. He does not water it. In March it flowers, one small yellow flower, and he stands in the door looking at it with his hands behind him, like a man who has been insulted.*

### 4

*He is their landlord. He had forgotten. The first of the month comes, and there is four hundred dollars owing on a trailer in lot fourteen.*

*He pays it. He pays it to his own holding company, out of his own pocket, in cash, and he has the receipt made out in their name. PAID. He does the same on the first of the month after.*

**V:** (They were proud of that receipt. They had it in a back pocket. They meant to put it down in front of me and say: on time.)

*The lot is not let again. He has the lock changed for a better, and he keeps no key.*

### 5

*He takes the four. It is the longest piece of work of his life, and he does it alone, and he tells each of them what it is for.*

*He is exact. Nothing is done that does not answer to something. Each of them asks what he gets out of it, and to each he gives the one word, and there is no smile with it.*

*It does not help. He had not supposed that it would. A debt paid to the wrong creditor is not discharged, and he was not the one who was owed. He does it thoroughly all the same, and the hounds eat for a month.*

### 6

*He finds that he has made the soup. It is noon. There is a pan of it on the stove and a tray laid, and he has been doing it for nine days at eight and noon and six without once looking at his hands.*

*He tastes it, standing. There is too much pepper. The onion has caught.*

**V:** (It is bad. They were quite right. I had put it down to malice.)

*He does not correct the recipe. He puts the bowl on the floor, and the old hound eats it, and looks up at him afterwards as that hound looks at a man who has been ill.*

### 7

*On War Day he goes to the bowling alley. Angel has kept the lane. Nobody bowls. Ronin reads the accords out, all four clauses, to an empty chair, and they let him.*

*Afterwards V goes out to the car park alone. It is where they said that they would do it. Somewhere stupid: they named it twice, and laughed.*

*He stands in the middle of it for an hour, in the open, under the lamp, with his back to the laundromat roof. It is the worst place in the city for a man in his trade to stand. He knows the range from that roof. He paced it in April.*

*Nothing happens. A car alarm goes off, and stops. At eleven he does up his coat and drives home at exactly the speed limit.*
