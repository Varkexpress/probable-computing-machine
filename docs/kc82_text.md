**V:** They were to be destroyed. Each of them had been used, by somebody, for something. I do not destroy what has been used. I destroy what uses.

**Misaki:** That is your whole thing, isn’t it? Used, and uses. Two columns.

**V:** It is the whole of it. You will have noticed which column I put you in.

**Misaki:** …Yes. I noticed.

**V:** I wrote that page before I had seen the films of your bones. I have not known since what to do with the page.

**V:** It is like accounts. I take no pleasure in the length of it. I take it in the sum coming out even.

**Misaki:** And mine? What does my sum come out to, when you do the accounts?

**V:** I have not been able to make it come out. I have told you that the page has no total.

**Misaki:** (He keeps saying that as if it were a confession. It is only arithmetic.)

**V:** A name on a shelter door is a man buying a good opinion of himself. I want the roof to be there. I do not want to be thanked by people who had no choice but to come in under it.

*You think of forty-one thank-yous, in the good voice, for soup.*

**Misaki:** Yes. Okay. That one I understand.

**V:** Three and a half hours.

**Misaki:** And what do you do with the rest of the night?

**V:** I read. I walk them. Lately I listen to a house with a second person in it. It is louder than I had expected.

**Misaki:** I am not loud. I’m practically furniture.

**V:** You count, under your breath. Exits. I can hear it from the stair.

**V:** A veterinary text, and eleven animals that did not live. I am better now. You have had the benefit of it.

**Misaki:** You practised on dogs. To get good enough for people.

**V:** I practised in order to save them. It is the only reason I have ever learned to do anything.

*He looks at your hand when he says it, and then, very deliberately, at the wall.*

**V:** Yes.

**Misaki:** Just “yes”. No speech.

**V:** You asked, and you watch hands; you would know if I lied. Yes. I would have done it that night. I would have been wrong, and I would never have found it out.

**Misaki:** (He said wrong. He said that he would have been wrong.)

**Misaki:** (…That is about that night. It says nothing about tomorrow. Don’t.)

**V:** It goes into the furnace, unread. Angel has the instruction.

**Misaki:** All of it? All those names, and not one person reads them?

**V:** It is not a record for anybody. It is how I know that I did not do any of it for nothing.

**Misaki:** And my page goes into the furnace with the card man.

**V:** I am making other arrangements for your page.

*He stops with the kettle in his hand.*

**V:** Nobody has asked me that in this house.

**Misaki:** Well, I’m asking, V. Don’t make it weird.

**V:** Whatever does not need cutting.

**Misaki:** (He is still on about the chop. He will be on about the chop at my funeral.)

**Misaki:** Because then I would have to decide. You get to decide, V; you have a house and a book. I have four hundred a month.

**Misaki:** Deciding is for people who can afford to be wrong about it.

**V:** (That is an answer. I had forty pages on them, and I did not have that.)

**Misaki:** My birthday, probably. I wrote it on an enrolment form myself, because March was the next month.

**Misaki:** Nobody wrote the real one down anywhere.

**V:** I have it in the file as a question mark.

**Misaki:** Yes. So have I.

**Misaki:** Exits. Always. If a room has fewer than two, I do not sit down in it.

**V:** There are three from the spare room.

**Misaki:** I know; I counted. You put me in the one room up there with three.

**V:** Yes.

*They wait for you to say why. You find that you cannot do it without saying something else as well, and you say nothing.*

**Misaki:** A pilot. Don’t you dare laugh.

**V:** I do not laugh.

**Misaki:** I had four thousand dollars in a biscuit tin, and the flight school wanted a birth certificate. So: this.

**V:** (On the ninth floor of a building that I own, a man sold birth certificates at three thousand apiece.)

**V:** (I let him the office.)

**Misaki:** No.

**Misaki:** …It did not. It does a little, now, and that is your fault. You keep saying him.

**Misaki:** He was a man in the way. Now he is a man whose wife needed teeth, and I know about the teeth.

**V:** Good.

**Misaki:** Fuck off with your “good”.

**V:** I did not say that I enjoyed it. I said good.

**Misaki:** A rich man with a hobby, who had never once been hungry and thought that made him clean.

**V:** And now?

**Misaki:** A rich man with a hobby who cuts up my dinner. Do not push it.

**Misaki:** Yes.

**Misaki:** That week, yes. I had it: two pounds on a three-pound trigger, and your ear in the glass.

**V:** Thank you.

**Misaki:** For what, exactly?

**V:** For not saying otherwise. I would have known, and then I could not have believed the next thing that you told me.

**Misaki:** In general, or do you mean for dinner?

**V:** Either.

**Misaki:** Nobody has asked me the first one before. For dinner: noodles.

**V:** And the first?

**Misaki:** …Ask me again when I have two working hands.

**V:** (I told them once what I would do when they had two hands. I do not think that they have forgotten it. I think that is why they said it.)
